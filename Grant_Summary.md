MTFS Holodeck — Grant Summary
One-sentence proposal
MTFS Holodeck is an open-source experimental sandbox for studying safe AI agency: an AI can observe a complex dynamical world, propose bounded interventions, measure consequences, and learn when not to act without experimenting directly on the real world.
The problem
As AI systems become more agentic, safety research needs environments where agents can explore consequences without requiring irreversible real-world experimentation.
The central research question is:
Can an AI learn state-dependent intervention policies while its actions remain bounded, observable, reversible, and experimentally testable?
What has already been built
The prototype includes:
constrained action types
safety validation
magnetic-dipole dynamics
geometric state representation
energy and geometry activity metrics
coherence and magnetic alignment
phase and attractor measurements
persistent-regime detection
passive/random/protective/aggressive control experiments
Rabi–Bloch dynamical testing
matched causal-window controls
explicit falsification experiments
Preliminary evidence
The current simulator produced persistent high-(S_{EG}) geometry-dominant episodes.
A protective WATCH policy preserved long-lived episodes at approximately passive levels, while random/aggressive intervention shortened episodes and reduced the separation state.
Blind detection experiments also found measurable short-horizon precursors to strong energy–geometry separation.
A causal-window experiment initially suggested an apparent timing advantage. A refined continuous-drive experiment matched the baseline and modulation energy budgets and showed that the apparent advantage did not generalize.
This is treated as a positive methodological result: the sandbox exposed a protocol-dependent effect rather than allowing an attractive hypothesis to become an unsupported control rule.

Scientific boundary
MTFS Holodeck is experimental research software. Its physical-inspired models are simulation substrates for AI-safety experiments and are not presented as independently validated physical or quantum technologies.
